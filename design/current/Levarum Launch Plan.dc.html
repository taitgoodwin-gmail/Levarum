Which artifact is the product?

Two half-products. The repo has the deployment vehicle, the real backend and the console workflow, wearing last season's clothes. This panel has the design truth and the richer public site, with no way to run a server. Neither is the product alone.
POSITION · RECOMMENDED
The repo is the product. This panel is where the product gets designed.
The repo is the deployment vehicle and backend of record — it is the only one of the two that can hold a domain, a serverless function and real auth. This panel is the design system and page source of record — layout, colour, copy and flow decisions happen here first, get proven on the rendered pages, and travel to the repo through one extracted bridge document. The alternative — treating this panel as the product and the repo as a renderer — fails on the goal: "nothing fake in the chain" is unreachable in an environment with no server. The alternative in the other direction — designing directly in the repo — throws away a working design-review loop (Site Check, Journeys, the tint ladder work) for no gain. Split the custody; never the editing.
The anti-drift rule: a file is edited in exactly one place. When a page has been ported, its .dc.html here becomes a design sketchpad for the next change — and every accepted change re-crosses the bridge by hand. Dual-editing is how the repo grew MindLever fossils in the first place.

The six gates

Each is a choice, not a task. Recommendations attached; overrule any of them, but in writing.
GATE 1 · LOGO MARK
Mark A or mark B?
Recommend B — the dome-on-plate already on every live page, consistent in Site Check, and it survives the 16px favicon squint. Switching now buys nothing and costs a seven-file sweep plus the repo favicon.
GATE 2 · PRICING
What does the cost FAQ actually say?
Recommend publishing a floor and a typical range — "first builds usually land between $X and $Y, agreed in writing before any work." The current answer ("agreed on the call") is what expensive sounds like to a tradesman; he quotes jobs for a living and respects anyone who will name a number. Only you can set X and Y. Dollars — this is a US national site.
GATE 3 · LEAVE-HOME RECOVERY
Capture him before he leaves, or let him go?
Recommend letting him go at launch — no exit popup, no early email ask; both would spend the trust the whole design is built to earn. Instead measure the drop-off (metric 1) for two weeks. If Home loses more than ~2 in 3, add the softest recovery there is: a phone number. Which is Gate 6's cousin — a number in the header recovers people no form ever will.
GATE 4 · GONE QUIET
Three tries, no answer — then what?
Recommend: Park it, one revival note at 90 days, then Not a fit. Needs the two new console statuses from P3 so "parked" stops masquerading as "done". Every lead reaches a named conclusion — which is half the goal sentence.
GATE 5 · DUAL RUST ON HOME
Nav Start and hero CTA share the first viewport.
Recommend keeping both. The 10% rule is about meaning, not census: both buttons mean the same verb, pointed at the same place — reinforcement, not competition. What the rule forbids is rust meaning two different things, which is what we actually fixed. Demoting nav Start would weaken every other page to tidy one viewport.
GATE 6 · ONE NAME AT THE DOOR
operator@levarum.co or owner / levarum?
Recommend operator@levarum.co, everywhere, once. It is already the repo's identity in brand.ts, it is a real mailbox shape that server auth can verify, and the console user is an operator whoever owns the company. The owner/levarum demo credential dies with the client gate in P3 — same decision, one hat.

FASTEST

Why he doesn't book

The 55-year-old plumber, in the van, on his phone, between jobs. Arguing against our own site, hardest objection first. Each card names the page that fails him and the change.
1"I'm not typing my email into a website I found an hour ago."FAILS ON · START, THE GATE
The gate is the funnel's single biggest wall, and he is exactly who bounces off it: email means salesmen. Change: soft-gate the plan — show the top job and its hours free, gate the remaining jobs and the fix-first pick behind the email. He gets proof before the ask; we still get the lead when he wants the rest. Metric 3 exists to prove or kill this.
2"There's no phone number. What are they hiding?"FAILS ON · EVERY PAGE
His whole trade runs on phone calls; a business with no number reads as a business that hides. Change: a real number in the footer of all five pages and "or just call" beside the booking slots on Start. It doubles as the Gate 3 recovery. Needs the number to exist — owner input.
3"Zapier? Claude? HubSpot? This is for computer people."FAILS ON · WHAT WE AUTOMATE
QuickBooks he knows. The rest of the tool chips are software-world names that mark the page as not-for-him — the exact "software, not workwear" failure the palette was rebuilt to avoid, re-imported through copy. Change: lead with the outcome ("runs on tools you already have — nothing new to learn"), demote tool names to the card's small print.
4"Show me one guy like me this worked for."FAILS ON · HOME
The example plan card is hypothetical and the dark band only names his pain — Journeys already flagged it as resolving no objection. There is no person, no trade, no town anywhere on the site. Change: replace the dark band's three abstractions with one named story — "Two vans, Toledo. Invoices chased themselves within a week" — the moment a real first client exists. Until then, say so plainly ("early days — you'd be among the first, which is why the plan is free"); honesty beats a fabricated case study with this audience every time.
5"'Agreed on the call' means it costs too much to print."FAILS ON · QUESTIONS
He quotes jobs for a living; a dodge on price is disqualifying, not intriguing. Change: Gate 2's published range, plus the comparison he actually runs in his head: what the hours cost him at his own call-out rate versus the build price. The Start plan already computes his hours — Questions should use them.
6"What's an intake?"FAILS ON · COPY, SITE-WIDE
"Game Plan" earns its keep — it sounds like something a tradesman would want. "Intake", "automation stack", "back office" are our words, not his. Change: a fastest-tier copy sweep with one rule — every noun must survive being said aloud across a counter. "The three questions", "paperwork", "the jobs you shouldn't be doing".

Five numbers for week one

Vercel Analytics custom events for launch week — zero build, good enough. A five-line /api/event replaces it only if we outgrow it. Each number answers one question; together they locate the leak.
1
Start rate — does Home earn the click?
Sessions reaching Start step 1 ÷ Home sessions. Hooks: hero CTA and nav Start clicks. Under ~1 in 3, Home is the leak and Gate 3 reopens.
2
Completion — do the three questions hold him?
Step 1 started → plan revealed. Hook: step-transition events in the prospect flow. This should be the strongest number on the board; if it isn't, the form is lying about taking 90 seconds.
3
Gate conversion — the number that decides the soft-gate argument
Plan built → email given. Hook: the unlock event, which in P3 is the /api/submissions POST itself — the one metric measured server-side, so it is also the one we trust most.
4
Booking rate — the goal, literally
Submissions with a slot ÷ all submissions, read straight off the store (slot non-null). No client hook needed once the endpoint is real.
5
Speed to contact — the operator's number, not the site's
Median hours from New → Contacted. Hook: status changes get a timestamp in updateSubmission (one-line change). The site can convert perfectly and still lose every lead here; this is the number that keeps the second half of the goal honest.
