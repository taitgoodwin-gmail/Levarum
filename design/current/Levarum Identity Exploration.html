Clean-slate exploration

Levarum identity

Four survivors from eight concepts, tested against ten structural properties. No lever anywhere.

Strategic brief

For: the owner of a small trades or home-services business, reading a phone in a truck between jobs. Not a design buyer. The mark will meet him on a van door, a shirt, an invoice and a 16px tab.

The one idea: something is taken off your plate — the week comes back lighter than it went out.

Must be: plain-spoken, sturdy, unmistakable at a glance.
Must not be: clever, technical, startup-fashionable.

Judgement rule for everything below: a contractor should be able to describe the mark to his mate in one sentence, and a sign shop should be able to cut it out of vinyl without asking a question.

The options

1 · The Week

Phone test: "A solid circle with one of its seven slices taken out."

Construction

One circle, radius 28 on a 64 unit square, centred. The circle is divided into seven equal 51.43° sectors; exactly one sector is removed, its bisector locked to the horizontal axis so the notch always points right. Nothing else exists in the mark: one radius, one angle, one subtraction.

512px

Drawn at 512 in the file, shown here at 320 to fit the page. The geometry is resolution-free — there is nothing in it that only works large.

Honest weaknesses

  • The pie-chart reading is unavoidable. A slice removed from a disc is the universal chart idiom, and some people will see data before they see a week.
  • A shallow notch is the first thing lost to bad reproduction — a cheap embroidery digitiser or a low-resolution favicon will round it away and leave a plain dot.
  • It shares its idea — subtraction — with option 2, so these two compete for the same slot rather than offering different strategies.

2 · The Notch

Phone test: "A solid square with a round bite out of the top right corner."

Construction

A 60 unit square on a 64 unit field. One circle, radius 24, centred exactly on the top-right corner point, subtracted. Two elements, one boolean operation, no optical adjustment anywhere: the bite radius is always 40% of the square's side.

3 · The Lift

Phone test: "A filled dome floating just above a solid bar."

Construction

One circle, radius 26, cut on its horizontal diameter: the upper half is kept. Below it, a bar of the same 52 unit width and 9 unit height, separated by a gap of 8 units — the gap is always 0.9 of the bar height, so the lift is visible before it is measured. Two elements, one radius, one gap ratio. From levare: to lift.

  • It is the least literal of the three. It carries "lighter" well and "your week" not at all.

4 · Lifted V — wordmark route

Phone test: "The word Levarum with the v sitting a step above the other letters."

Construction

No symbol. Poppins SemiBold, tracking −0.035em, and one rule: the v is raised by 0.14 of the cap height and nothing else changes. The raise is the same fraction at every size, so the word scales as one object. The letter that gets lifted is the letter that starts levare.

Verdict: it does not hold up at 14px, and it is marginal at 16px. Lora's moderate stroke contrast and fine serif terminals thin out at small sizes, and this reader is one-handed, in daylight, often at arm's length on a dusty screen. Question labels at 16px are acceptable; 14px helper text and dropdown options are not. Smallest fix, for you to accept or reject — I have not applied it: restrict Lora to prose at 17px and above, set all interface text (labels, helper, options, buttons, table cells) in Poppins 500 with a 15px floor. That is a scope change, not a pairing change — Lora stays, and stays where it is good.

Clear space

Clear space on all four sides equals 1.8 × the bar height — the bar is the one element every reproduction keeps, so the rule is measurable off the artwork itself with no reference to the original file.

Minimum size

MediumMinimumWhy
Screen16px tallBelow this the 2px gap closes on low-density displays and the mark becomes one mass.
Print — offset or digital6mm tallKeeps the gap above 0.8mm, clear of dot gain.
Embroidery, etch, vinyl12mm tallStitch and blade minimums close small gaps; below 12mm order the gap widened to 1.4 × bar height as a separate production artwork.

Misuse — five things someone will actually do to this mark

1 · Closing the gap

Someone tidies the "floating" piece down onto the bar. The gap is the idea; without it the mark says nothing.

2 · Outlining it

Redrawn as thin strokes to match an icon set. It then fails silhouette and disappears at small sizes.

3 · Gradient sunrise

A gradient turns the dome into a setting sun and dates the mark instantly.

4 · Two-colour split

Colouring the two parts differently breaks property 3 and makes single-colour production impossible.

5 · Tilting it

Rotated for "energy". The bar is a ground line; tilted, the thing being lifted looks like it is sliding off.

Motion

One entrance, once. The bar is already in place; the dome rises 9 units into position over 620ms on a decelerating curve, at 35% to 100% opacity. Nothing loops, nothing bounces. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the dome renders in its final position with no animation and no fade — tested in this file, not assumed.

What I did not test or verify

  • Font rendering. This file makes no external requests, so Poppins and Lora render only if installed locally; otherwise you are seeing fallbacks (Century Gothic / Futura and Georgia / Iowan). The small-size verdict above is based on Lora's construction and my rendering here — repeat it on a real phone with the real webfonts before you act on it.
  • Licence primary sources. Read from secondary listings, not from each foundry's own OFL file. Marked GAP above.
  • Physical reproduction. No proof printed, no vinyl cut, no stitch-out. The 6mm and 12mm minimums are conventional starting figures, not measured results from your suppliers.
  • Trademark clearance. No search of any register for the name Levarum or for any of these marks. Both the dome-over-bar and the notched-circle geometries are simple enough that prior registrations are plausible.
  • Real-world recognition. No one but me has been shown these, and the phone test is only valid when an actual stranger sketches it. Three people, one sentence each, is a twenty-minute test worth running before you commit.
  • Colour on substrate. Contrast ratios are computed for sRGB screens only — not for print on uncoated stock, not for a van wrap in sunlight.
  • The rest of the site. No file in the prototype was touched, and nothing here has been applied to it. The diagnostic audit I had started is unfinished and is not included in this file.
